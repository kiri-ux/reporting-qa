"WHAT WAS UPDATED?" - THE BANNER COULD NOT TELL YOU
===================================================
"A deploy changed the checking rules" is true of every one of those banners and
answers nothing, so the only way to find out what was actually different was to
ask me. It names the build and prints that build's own note now.

And the honest answer for the one you were looking at: NOTHING about how a
report is judged. Build 177 changed when the sweeper starts - a sleep - and 880
reports were queued for a full re-read because of it.

That is my fault twice over in two days. 172 put the whole of recheck.py into
the fingerprint to fix reports that a parser fix could not reach; too narrow
became too wide, and every timing tweak in that file now costs an afternoon of
pdftotext for no change of answer.

Only the two functions that decide what a stored report ends up SAYING are in
it now - the one that writes the answers back, and the one the pair check
compares against. Changing the pacing, the claim, or when the sweep starts does
not queue anything.


WHICH DAYS ARE IN THE DAILY SERVE FILE
======================================
You asked where to see this. Nowhere, until now: the panel said "1,070 clients
loaded for August 2026", which is equally true of a file holding one day and a
file holding thirty-one - and a client is owed a report at seven days, so the
difference between those two files is the difference between "ran" and "did
not" on hundreds of rows.

The orders page shows the month as a strip of thirty-one squares, filled for a
day the file has and red for one it does not, with the client count on hover:

  24 of 31 days of August 2026 are in the file, 2026-08-01 to 2026-08-31.
  Nothing on 2026-08-03, 2026-08-10, 2026-08-17. A day with no delivery on it
  anywhere is usually a file that did not arrive rather than a day nobody ran.

The dates were already being kept per client - this is the union of them, so it
is a reading of what is loaded rather than a new thing to maintain.
