DELETING THE S3 FILES WILL NOT HELP
======================================
Different disk. The S3 bucket is Amazon's; /data is Render's, attached to the
service. Nothing you delete in one frees a byte in the other.

/data holds three things: the report PDFs, the packaged zips, and the order
downloads. It is the third one that did this.


WHAT FILLED IT
=================
The sync downloads the export to a temp directory under /data and removes it
afterwards - on the success path and on the failure path. Neither of those
runs if the PROCESS is killed. A deploy, a restart, the OOM killer: any of
them mid-sync leaves the whole download on the disk, forever, and the next
sync makes a fresh one beside it.

That is a leak that only shows up as somebody else's error. "Downloaded but
could not import: OSError [Errno 28]" is not about the file - the file is
fine. It is the disk being full, reported by whatever happened to be writing
when it ran out.

Three things now:

  * EVERY SYNC SWEEPS ABANDONED DOWNLOADS FIRST. Anything named orders-* and
    older than half an hour goes. Half an hour so a sync running right now in
    the other worker keeps its own files.

  * THE PRUNE TAKES THE OLD PACKAGED ZIPS TOO. One is built per partner per
    package, and "sync reports" builds another - so a cycle re-packaged a few
    times leaves several copies of every partner's reports on the disk, on top
    of the reports themselves. Past the retention window they are two copies
    of a copy. Deleted with the PDFs of the same month.

  * THE ERROR SAYS IT IS THE DISK when it is the disk, with the free space,
    instead of sending somebody to look at a file that parses perfectly well.

And the orders page carries a red panel past 75% full, saying how much is
left and that the S3 bucket is a different disk.


WHAT TO DO NOW
=================
Deploy this and press Sync from S3. The sweep runs first, so if abandoned
downloads are what filled it, the sync that clears them is the same one that
then succeeds.

If it is still tight afterwards, the orders page will say so, and the next
thing to look at is KEEP_PDF_MONTHS - the report PDFs are the bulk of the
rest, roughly 1.6 GB a cycle, and the archive in the shared drive is the copy
that matters by then.
