McNUTT SITE SERVICES - THE TOP LINE CAME OFF PAGE NINE
======================================================
Page one says 54,544 impressions and 3,303 clicks. The one line item says
exactly the same. The report was failed twice anyway, "against a stated 10" and
"against a stated 2".

The pattern that finds the top line searches with DOTALL and a lazy `.*?`,
which means it will walk the entire document looking for anything that fits its
shape. It walked twenty-four THOUSAND characters - past the tiles, past the
line items - and matched "10  2  20%" out of a month-over-month comparison
table nine pages later. Then it compared the line item against that.

Two things wrong, both fixed:

  * A page-one tile is on page one. The search is bounded to the label's own
    window and stops dead at the page break.
  * The tiles are not always two across. This report has an Events tile, so the
    numbers do not land on one line in the order the old pattern assumed - page
    one reads "5,343" on its own line and then "54,544   3,303". It reads by
    column now, which is what the eye does: the number under the Clicks label
    is the clicks.

It says NO CTR on a report like this rather than the wrong percentage. There is
no CTR tile - the 9.80% beside those two is the Event Rate - and answering with
it would put every CTR rule against a different number entirely.

Every report that was reading correctly still reads the same. There is a test
holding all seven, and this PDF is now a fixture.


AND THE SPEND HAD NO DOLLAR SIGN ON IT
======================================
"Performance Max Cost - Amount spent on the campaign - 1,800.36". No sign. The
reader wanted one, found nothing, and the panel said "-/$2,000 no comparison"
about a report printing the spend in ninety-point type.

A bare number counts as money under a tile whose label is already known to be a
cost, still matched by column - so it cannot wander off and read an impression
count as dollars. McNutt now paces 1,800.36 against 2,000: 10% short.

Whole report re-checked end to end: clean pass.


AND THE FIX COULD NOT REACH THE NUMBERS
=======================================
That is the one I should have caught yesterday, and it is the reason the
failure was still there after 170.

A background re-check rewrites a report's findings, its severity and its
products - and leaves impressions and clicks exactly as the import first read
them. So a fix to a RULE reaches every stored report, and a fix to the READER
reaches none of them.

McNutt's lifetime was stored at 10 impressions and 2 clicks by the parser that
walked nine pages past the tiles. 170 fixed the parser. The lifetime re-checked
and came back clean - and its stored numbers stayed 10 and 2, because nothing
writes them. Then the monthly, which is compared against the lifetime, read
those numbers and said:

  This month prints impressions 54,544 against 10; clicks 3,303 against 2 on
  the lifetime.

A real finding on a real report, made entirely of a number nothing in the
system could correct.

A re-check writes impressions, clicks and the page count back now. And when
they change it marks the OTHER half of the pair stale, because the sweep has no
idea which order it will reach the two in - fixing the lifetime while the month
still carries a finding about the old numbers is only half a fix.

Nothing to press. The sweep will re-read both.


AND 171 COULD NOT REACH THE REPORTS EITHER
==========================================
Which is why the failure was still on your screen with 171 in the footer.

A report re-checks itself when the fingerprint of the checking code changes.
That fingerprint hashes app/checks/ and nothing else. 171 changed only
recheck.py - so the fingerprint did not move, nothing was queued, and no report
ever ran the fix. It was sitting in the build doing nothing.

That is the same shape as a bug this tool already has a paragraph about
elsewhere: the fingerprint was too narrow by exactly the thing it was written
for. recheck.py decides what a re-check WRITES BACK, so a change to it has to
reach the stored reports just as much as a change to a rule does. It is in the
fingerprint now, with a test that fails if it ever falls out again.

Installing this queues every report, and this time the fix runs.

If you do not want to wait for the sweep on McNutt: press "Check this file
again" on the LIFETIME first, then on the monthly. In that order - the lifetime
is where the wrong 10 and 2 are stored, and the monthly is only repeating them.
