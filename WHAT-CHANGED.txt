The generic logo
------------------
A report going out with the reporting tool's default blue chart icon instead of
the station's logo is now a failure: "The report is using the reporting tool's
default logo", on page 1.

HOW IT KNOWS, without a list of 146 partner logos to keep current:

The top-left corner of page one is rendered, downsampled and hashed, and the
hash is stored on the report. Then the test is who the logo BELONGS to -

    a partner's logo    appears on that partner's reports
    a client's logo     appears on that client's report
    a generic logo      appears on everybody's

A mark found on three or more different markets is not any of theirs. It learns
which image that is from the reports as they arrive, and needs nothing
maintained.

Three markets, not two, on purpose: a partner group really can cover two
markets and print one logo across both, and at two that group would be accused
of using a template.

The client's own logo is safe by the same rule - it is on one client's report,
so it never reaches three markets.

Details:

  - Downsampled to 96x32 greyscale before hashing, because the same logo
    rendered by two builds of poppler can differ by a pixel of anti-aliasing
    and a full-size hash would call those two different logos.
  - The crop takes the corner only - it stops well short of the centred
    "Digital Marketing Report" and the date block on the right, so a change of
    client name or date cannot move the fingerprint.
  - If the corner cannot be read at all - no poppler, no page one - the check
    abstains and shows as skipped rather than failing the report.
  - 0.2s per report, on ingest and on re-check.

Investigate lists the other markets carrying the same mark, so a false positive
would name itself.
