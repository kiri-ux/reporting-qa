THE MINUTE, AND THE SCROLLING BACK
=====================================
Both had the same cause, and it was mine.

Approve and reject posted the decision and then REDIRECTED BACK TO THE AUDIT
PAGE - which re-runs the whole comparison to change one cell: the board rebuilt
from every order line, the pasted list parsed again, every reason worked out
from scratch. That is the minute. And a fresh page starts at the top, which is
the scrolling.

The page saves the decision in place now. No reload, nothing rebuilt, the row
turns into its answer where it sits and the page does not move. Working down a
list of forty is forty clicks instead of forty clicks and forty scrolls.

Without JavaScript the form still posts and still redirects, exactly as before -
and if the save fails for any reason it falls back to that rather than losing
what you decided.


HOW OFTEN IT SYNCS WITH THE BREAKOUT SHEET: IT WAS NOT ON A CLOCK
====================================================================
The honest answer about the build you are on. The sheet is read on every order
sync, and the order sync only ever ran on two things:

  a batch of reports arriving
  somebody pressing "Sync from S3 now"

During the pull that is constant. On the 12th of the month, when nothing is
arriving, it is never - so a reporter changing hands in the sheet reached the
board whenever somebody next happened to press something. That is not a
schedule. Same for the daily serve file, which is worse: it lands every morning
and nothing was going to look at it until the next batch.

NOW IT IS EVERY THIRTY MINUTES. All three outside sources ride the same
heartbeat, and each is checked by fingerprint before anything is read:

  the order export        by ETag - a HEAD request, no download
  the daily serve file    by its own ETag, separately
  the breakout sheet      by a checksum of its own contents

The usual answer is "nothing has changed", which costs three small requests and
no writes. One worker does it: the sync claims itself, so the second worker's
heartbeat finds it taken and goes back to sleep. And it cannot die quietly - a
failed check is logged and the next one runs half an hour later, because a
heartbeat that stops on one bad answer is worse than none at all.

The Partners page states the interval rather than leaving you to guess.
SYNC_EVERY_MINUTES changes it, 0 turns it off, and the sync log now says "the
scheduled check - every half hour, whether or not anything is arriving" so a
sync nobody started is not a mystery.
