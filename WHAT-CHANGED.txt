THE 2018 CAME FROM A COLUMN THAT IS NOT A DATE
=================================================
You were right that there is no 2018 on that order. It is not on it, and the
tool was not reading the order - it was reading two columns that do not hold
what their names say.

Here is the evidence, from your four sample files. orders_end_date, every row:

  ordersdbrethink           665 rows    2026-12-31
  ordersdbstephensmedford   212 rows    2026-12-31
  ordersdb7moupa            444 rows    2026-12-31
  ordersdbanne               83 rows    2026-12-31

1,404 rows, four partners, five orders, ONE value. That is the end of the
range the export was pulled over. It is not any campaign's end date.

And its partner column is no better. Order 36184's header says the order
started 2024-02-07 while its own line item started 2018-01-01 - six years
before the order it belongs to. Manning's 55987 came through headed
2018-03-21 to 2018-05-18 against a line item that ran 29 June to 31 July 2026.
Neither of those is a date about that order.

WHAT BELIEVING THEM COST, beyond the pull list. An order end of 2026-12-31 on
every campaign in the business means no campaign ever finishes in the month
being reported - so no lifetime is ever owed by that path, and every row reads
as running to the end of next year. That has been quietly true since the new
export went in.

BOTH COLUMNS ARE NOW SET ASIDE. Detected, not hard-coded: one distinct order
end date across several orders is a window bound; several values are dates. If
the export starts carrying real ones this starts using them again, and the
line in the sync message saying it happened disappears on its own.


WHAT REPLACED THEM
=====================
The header was standing in for one thing: how far back does this ORDER reach.
It matters because a lifetime covers the whole order, and the line items that
reach furthest back are exactly the ones dropped at import for having finished
years ago - so their start was lost and the header was the only trace left.

That fact is now taken from the rows themselves, collected BEFORE the skip:
the earliest start among the order's own line items, whether or not they
survive the import.

  ReThink 4701      one line item, 2018-11-01     -> 2018-11-01, unchanged
  Hilbing 36184     lines 2018-01-01, 2020, 2021  -> 2018-01-01, was 2024-02-07
  Manning 55987     one line item, 2026-06-29     -> 2026-06-29, was 2018-03-21
  W&L 14885         lines 2020-08-10 and 2021     -> 2020-08-10 on both rows

The last one is the case the header existed for, now working off real rows: the
Social Mirror line starts in February 2021, and the pull still reaches back to
August 2020 because another line item on that order does.

The sync message says when it happens, and the orders page shows it.
