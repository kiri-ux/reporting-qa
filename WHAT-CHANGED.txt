Budgets, without re-pulling the whole export
----------------------------------------------
There is a BUDGETS panel on the Orders page now. Drop the Performance Max sheet
in and it fills the money columns on the lines it names.

IT MERGES, IT DOES NOT REPLACE. That is the whole point and it is worth being
explicit: your 368-row PMax file put through the normal import would have
deleted every order for every other product on the board and left PMax
standing. This one reads, matches and updates - it never deletes a row and
never creates one, so dropping in a single-product sheet is safe.

  - Matched on line item id, falling back to order id.
  - Reads Monthly Campaign Budget, and the per-product spend columns for Meta,
    PPC and LinkedIn.
  - Anything in the sheet that is not on the board yet is counted and reported
    rather than quietly dropped - that is the sheet being ahead of the sync,
    and it is worth seeing.
  - Where one order has several flights, the last row wins. A client running
    one product across two flights has two rows and the later one is what this
    month is paced against.

Run against your file on a scratch database: 368 rows read, every one carrying
a budget, matching correctly on both paths and leaving unrelated lines alone.


The display spelling of the export
------------------------------------
Your sheet uses the IO tool's display names - "Order's Status", "Client
Business Unit", "Monthly Campaign Budget". The nightly S3 file uses snake_case.
Same columns, and the reader only knew one spelling, so a perfectly good file
would have been rejected with a confusing message about not looking like an
export. It now takes either.

The main import also reads the four money columns when they are present, so
when you do re-pull, the budgets come with it and the panel becomes a
stopgap you no longer need.

One thing this nearly broke, caught by a test: the export carries two
`end_date` columns, and the second is the one that has always been read.
Normalising the headers silently switched it to the first, which changed which
rows survived the date filter. Last-one-wins is restored and pinned.
